Why v5 read as a document — the visual audit

1 · The type contrast was too flat. A 22px hero over 14px body is a 1.57× jump, which is the ratio of a magazine page, not a product moment. Nothing on the card performed. 2 · Colon labels are form fields. "Visual:", "Topic:", "Take:" — six of them on the idea card. Sentence-casing did not fix it because the colon-label pattern itself is the memo idiom. 3 · One uniform left-set text column with a single grey thumbnail and zero tonal variation is, literally, a printed page. 4 · The voice was archival. "Borrowed from", "Seed:", "Written for" is a librarian cataloguing sources, not a strategist handing you a move. 5 · Uniform rhythm: every zone had the same leading and the same gaps, so the eye had no landing sequence.

The two answers below attack all five, differently. Stage treats the card as a performance: one oversized voice moment with air around it, then authored prose where the scan anchor is the bolded first words of the sentence ("Open on a crash-zoom…"), which is how ChatGPT and Claude format answers. No labels, no colons, no icons. Studio treats the card as a product object: no text labels anywhere, each fact-group anchored by a small muted glyph, the source a tappable Case-study object. The Linear and Perplexity idiom. Both carry the full wording pass.

The calls

1 · Stage or Studio — or Stage's prose with Studio's Case-study object, which compose cleanly if you want the hybrid.

2 · "Proven structure" or "Case study" for the source object; both are mocked, one per direction.

3 · The "Visual" label lock. Both directions replace it ("Open on…" / the camera glyph). Bless the relaxation or it goes back as a bold lead-in ("Visual: crash-zoom…").

4 · Quotes on the hook hero — Stage only. Say if the marks feel right for a line meant to be spoken.
