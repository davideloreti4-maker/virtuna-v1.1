Why the audience line comes back, with a different field in it

You were right that “Written for The Aspirant” says nothing. It prints a display name that is either the creator's own label or our derived vocabulary for a slug like lurker. It is a category, and a category is not information.

But the persona it names carries a field the card has never shown: reaction_frame (enrich-signature.ts:191), described in the schema as “how THIS audience's segment judges content”. It is the only persona text the writer is ever given (select-persona-targets.ts:81). It is literally the brief this hook was written against. Put it on the face and the line stops being a label and becomes the reason the hook looks the way it does.

It also stays honest. It states an aim, claims no measurement, and sets up the door instead of competing with it: the card says who this was built for and what makes them bite, the door says go find out whether they did.

The audience line, on its own

Four ways to write it, at card width. The data behind all of them is the same: archetype (already on the card), share (already on the card), and repaint/reaction_frame (real, calibrated, not currently on the card. This is the one field that needs adding to CardTargetSchema).

1 · Frame only · the name is gone, the share leads

Written for

34% of your audience who judge a video by whether it saves them a mistake they were about to make.

What it is: the version used in both cards above. The percentage is the only number on the card and it measures your audience, not the output, so it cannot be mistaken for a verdict. Risk: the segment has no handle, so a creator cannot say “that one again” across cards.

2 · Frame only, no label · prose, closest to Editorial

Written for the 34% of your audience who judge a video by whether it saves them a mistake they were about to make.

What it is: the same sentence with the label folded in, so it does not read as a form field. Fits the card better if the audience line ends up last rather than leading. Risk: loses the scan anchor that every other row on the card has.

3 · Name plus frame · the name earns its place by being explained

Written for

The Aspirant, 34% of your audience.

They judge a video by whether it saves them a mistake they were about to make.

What it is: you rejected the bare name, and this is the test of whether the name was the problem or the emptiness was. With the frame under it the name becomes a handle for a group the creator can now recognise. Risk: two lines for one fact, and the name is still ours, not theirs, unless they renamed the persona in the workspace.

4 · Frame plus proof · the strongest, and the one that needs a second field

Written for

34% of your audience who judge a video by whether it saves them a mistake they were about to make.

Save rate 4.0% against a 1.8% baseline.

What it is: every persona also carries evidence, an engagement-ratio proof derived from the creator's real account (enrich-signature.ts:191; real values look like save-rate 4% vs 1.8% baseline). It is the only thing on any of these cards computed from their own data rather than written by a model. Risk: it describes the segment, not this hook, so it repeats identically on every card aimed at the same person, and a number under an aim can read as a verdict about the hook. This is the moat made visible, and it is also the easiest thing here to mis-read.

The v3 handoff's headline was “every card is shorter than today”. Under your two choices that is no longer true, and it is worth knowing before you sign off rather than after. The cost splits cleanly in two, and they are independent:

1 · Dropping the left label column costs height, and only at 728px. Hook +94, idea +166. At 342px it costs nothing (it is actually ~30px cheaper, from a footer fix made here) because v3's direction B already stacked at that width. You have chosen to pay the mobile layout's height at desktop width too, in exchange for one layout instead of two.

2 · The audience line costs a flat 61px at 728 and 105px at 342, on every card that has one. That is one row carrying two to three lines of real prose, and it is the same cost on hook and idea.

Hook comes out level with what ships today (590 → 591 at 728, 694 → 751 at 342). Idea is the one that moves: +27% at 728 and +30% at 342. If that is too much, the cheapest ~120px back is folding “Topic” and “Format” into the lede sentence, since both are one-liners wearing a label. The next cheapest is putting the left column back at 728 only, which is the thing you just rejected.

What this costs, and what is still open

The audience line is the first thing in this rework that touches the engine

1. repaint (and evidence, for variant 4) must be added to CardTargetSchema in blocks.ts, and threaded through bindTarget plus the runners that build a target. Small and additive, but it is a schema change and the plan you approved touches zero engine files.

2. It only exists on hook and idea cards. Script targeting is dormant (script-runner.ts:632) so target is null on every route today, and remix-runner never emits target at all. Script and remix carry no audience evidence under any of the four variants. If that gap matters, waking script targeting is its own lane.

3. The frame is model-authored, so it will sometimes carry an em dash. Same category as the other model-written fields you chose to flag rather than fix.

Still unanswered from v3

The door on a legacy card that really did run a panel.
