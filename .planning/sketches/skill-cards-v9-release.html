What changed, and why this is the one

The ceiling was never the styling. It was the count. A Perplexity Discover card ships four elements: image, headline, a two-line dek, a source row. Claude ships an artifact tile; ChatGPT ships a canvas card. Ours was shipping eight elements, and no typography makes eight elements read premium. The big 3 do not delete detail, they stage it: sources, citations and tool results are all collapsed by default. v9 does exactly that. The face carries the case-study cover you picked, the line, one breath of why, and the source stats. The template, the audience, the shot notes and your seed line live behind one quiet "Full brief" row. Nothing is deleted; it is staged.

And the mock now carries production fidelity: composed covers that read as real video stills (in production these are the actual TikTok covers, on file for 516 of 532 corpus videos), button radius corrected to the system's 8px, spacing on the 4px grid, and the card shown in its real habitat: a stack of siblings.

The calls

1 · The staging call, the real one: the audience line, the template and the first-frame plan now live one tap away. Round 1's verdict was "deleting content regressed value"; this is staging, not deletion, and it is the only way to a four-element face. Bless it, or name the one fact that must stay on the face and it trades places with the source sentence.

2 · The dek is authored: it fuses the shot and the mechanism into two lines. That means one more small runner field (a 2-line summary), same additive shape as the mechanism name.

3 · Covers: production uses the real TikTok stills. When a cover is missing (16 of 532), the composed fallback shown here takes its place; the empty state above covers the no-source case entirely.
