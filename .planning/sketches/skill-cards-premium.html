Skill cards — the premium pass · sketch 2 · in-thread generation only

You are sitting on the cheatcode and printing it as a footnote.

Every grounded card already carries the most persuasive assets in the product: a real TikTok cover, a verified 144.9× vs follower baseline, two million measured views, and — the best of them — the source hook rendered as a fill-in-the-blank formula. That is a teardown of someone else's winner, handed over as a template. It is the single most "how is this legal" thing here.

Today all of it renders as a 34-pixel thumbnail in a grey strip, fifth down the card, in quieter chrome than the fabricated 7/10 above it. The card gives top billing to a number it invented and buries the numbers it earned. This pass inverts that, and rebuilds the four cards around one story every skill can tell: here is what worked · here is the pattern · here is yours.

The moves

Six changes. Most of them are subtraction.

01
Give the real number the big type
7/10 would stop at 13px → 144.9× at 30px, tabular, right-aligned. Range is what reads as expensive; the cards currently live entirely between 11 and 14px.
02
Promote the formula
hookTemplate already ships the source hook as "Stop [doing the hard thing]. Do [the easier thing] instead." It renders today as a small muted line inside the receipt. It is the cheatcode. Make it a zone.
03
Mono for instruments
Uppercase mono micro-labels for meta and stats — the language the v2 ambient surfaces already speak. Inter for content, Newsreader for voice. Three faces, three jobs, no overlap.
04
De-box completely
Zero nested bordered tiles. Today a hook card puts up to four bordered boxes inside a bordered card. Replaced by hairline-divided zones with a tone shift — the v2 grammar's own rule.
05
The cover at a real size
34×60px → 62px wide at true 9:16. A real video still is the most premium asset available and it is currently a stamp.
06
One story, four skills
Source → pattern → yours. Remix already tells it (that is why its map is the best thing on the surface). Hook, idea and script have the same data and don't. Now they all do.

The ungrounded case — it is not rare

All three variants above assume proof is present. It is present only when grounded generation was on and the model attributed this specific card to a real source carrying a handle. On an ungrounded run there is no cover, no multiplier and no formula — so the entire opening zone vanishes and A collapses into B.

That is the honest degrade and it is fine, but it means A's wow is conditional and B is what a meaningful share of cards will actually look like. Worth knowing before picking: A is the ceiling, B is the floor, and picking A means designing B anyway.

Script card

Make it read as a call sheet, because that is what it is.

The script card holds the most genuinely premium payload in the product — timed beats, a per-beat director cue, and a consolidated shoot plan. Most competitors return a paragraph. The problem is presentational: the filming cue renders in the same 12px muted grey as everything else, so a shot list reads like a disclaimer. Mono timings, a mono cue prefix and a real left column turn the same data into a document you could hand to someone.

Shooting script
5 beats · 40s · Talking-head + screen-record b-roll
00:00 Hook

I stopped editing my videos and my views tripled.

Close-up, handheld · text slams in on "tripled" · deadpan, no smile.
00:03 Setup

For two years I spent 6 hours per video in CapCut. Cuts, captions, zooms — the works.

B-roll of the CapCut timeline scrubbing · VO over the screen-record.
00:10 Turn

Then I noticed my raw, one-take clips were outperforming the polished ones 3 to 1.

Cut back to face · split-screen the two analytics graphs.
00:20 Payoff

Turns out the algorithm rewards watch-time, and over-editing was killing my pacing.

Medium shot · one bold card: "watch-time > polish" · slower.
00:35 CTA

Try one unedited take this week and tell me your retention. I bet it climbs.

Direct to camera · point at the comment area · warm, challenging.

Changes: timings become mono timecodes in a fixed left column (a script reads down a gutter, not across a row) · the connecting dot-rail goes, because the timecode column already carries the sequence and the rail was decoration · beat 1 takes the serif so the opener is the focal point without repeating it above · the filming cue keeps its camera glyph but now sits under a beat that has visible structure, so it reads as a cue rather than an afterthought · retentionMarker and the four-row shoot plan move to More, which is where you go once, not per beat.

The stack · what five of these feel like

Variety is the point. A stack of identical cards is a spreadsheet.

The owner's original note — "they all should have their value, atm they kinda look the same" — is a stack problem, not a card problem. Five cards that each open with the same grey box read as one repeated element. Here the source band varies by what each hook actually has: a big multiplier, a view count, or nothing at all. The variation is the data being honest, and it happens to be what makes the stack scan.
