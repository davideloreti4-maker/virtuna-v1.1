The v5 thesis

The rework's own reframe was: the problem was never too much information, it was that everything carried the same weight. v4 (Document, stacked) fixed the boxes and the verdict, then quietly reintroduced the disease: eight label-above-content rows on the idea card, every fact wearing the identical costume, at +27% height on desktop and +30% on mobile. A form weighs every field the same. That is what a form is.

The products this card is benchmarked against do not render answers as forms. They render answers: a strong typographic spine, supporting prose that self-reads, metadata set quiet and tight, citations as small media objects, chrome only at the frame. v5 does that with three weight tiers. Voice: the deliverable, serif, the one focal point. Craft: the two or three sentences that make it usable, 14px prose with sentence-case run-in labels. Ledger: everything factual, 13px, tight leading, grouped by whitespace, with the source as a citation object. One hairline on the whole card, at the footer.

The run-in label is the load-bearing move: Why it works: a pattern-interrupt… keeps direction B's scan anchor and costs zero vertical pixels, which is exactly what v4's stacked rows were paying ~90 to 170px per card for. Every locked decision holds: no verdict on the face, no boxes, no uppercase, no two-column interiors, the Visual label kept, angle leads the idea card, the door in the footer, room renamed audience, no em dashes.

Audit · what the shipped card actually does

Re-verified against source this session (hook-card-block.tsx, script-card-block.tsx, remix-card-block.tsx read end to end; findings 1–8 of the handoff hold).

1 · The verdict is one self-estimated number in five costumes. Band word, fraction, ribbon, invented quote and rank all derive from personaStops, which the writer emits about its own output (hooks-runner.ts:605–615). No sim ran. The locked decision strips all five from the face; the simulation owns the verdict.

2 · The targeting line is structurally dead. All three callers pass personas: [], so the receipt branch at target-reaction.tsx:45 has never rendered in production. What ships is an internal archetype name and a percentage. v4 and v5 both replace it with the persona's reaction_frame, the actual brief the hook was written against, which requires one additive schema change (CardTargetSchema).

3 · Chrome outnumbers content. The shipped hook face carries four nested bordered boxes, four uppercase labels, two pills, a caret and three hairlines for nine facts. The caret's contents (seed, delivery) are one quiet line of information wearing an expand interaction.

4 · "See the room →" opens a room of nobody on post-2026-07-22 cards: with personas absent the room renders viewer_1…viewer_10 placeholders and the grounding gate fails. The door must point at the audience simulation instead, which is the locked decision and what the footer door does in both v4 and v5.

Review · v4 against v5, honestly

What v4 got right and v5 keeps: no boxes, no uppercase, no verdict, the serif hero, the citation as a media object, the footer door, the rewritten audience line carrying the reaction frame instead of an archetype name, identical structure at both widths.

Where v4 falls short of the bar: a dl of equal rows is a spec sheet. The hook card gives "Seed" the same visual rank as the shot direction; the idea card needs eight rows and pays +171px at 728 for facts that are one line each. None of the reference products label a one-word fact with a dedicated row. And the height regression inverts the original brief, which was a card that reads lighter than today, not heavier.

What v5 borrows, and from where: run-in bold-label prose is how ChatGPT and Claude format structured answers inside running text. The citation object (content line + one muted attribution line with the single colored stat) is Perplexity's source idiom. The serif voice moment is already Virtuna's own. The single footer hairline is all three of them: chrome only at the frame.

v5's honest trade-offs: a run-in label is a weaker scan anchor than a stacked one; the eye finds it by color at the line start rather than by position in a column. On wrap, the second line returns to the left margin with no hanging indent, so long values briefly lose their label. The ledger cluster relies on proximity grouping, which is subtler than borders and depends on the 7px/22px spacing rhythm actually surviving implementation. If the owner wants stronger anchors on the idea card specifically, its recipe cluster is the one place a stacked label would still earn its height.
