The brief, open · where the detail went

One tap stages the template, the audience frame, the first-frame plan and the seed line as quiet cells. Nothing shipped on the old face is deleted except the self-estimated verdict, which the simulation door now owns.

Visual design review

Shipped counts against itself: the idea face shows 15 distinct visual elements in three different container styles (bordered boxes, a boxed one-liner, borderless labelled zones), five uppercase micro-labels, three pills in two shapes, and a verdict row whose colored dot, bold fraction and ribbon make it the highest-contrast element on the card — louder than the primary action. There is no focal sequence; every zone asks for attention with the same voice.

v9 has a focal sequence: cover → quoted serif line → dek → source stats → actions. One container style, one label, one accent use (the sage multiplier), and the only high-contrast element is the deliverable itself. The composition survives mobile unchanged, which the shipped recipe strip does not.

UX review

Shipped: the reading order is ambiguous and the card carries two competing forward paths ("See the room →" mid-card vs the primary button at the foot) plus a caret whose payload is one quiet line. The recipe strip clips the take with an ellipsis at every width — the one line the creator owns is the only cell that truncates. Mobile cards run 680–1400px.

v9: a single reading path, one primary action, the door in the footer, and detail behind a brief row that names its contents so nothing feels hidden. Collapsed cards are 448px on desktop and 405px on mobile. Honest risks: the brief costs one tap — if its open-rate is low, the take and the audience frame are effectively invisible; the dek is model-authored (a new runner field, so its quality varies); and a large source cover could momentarily read as the creator's own video, which the "Case study · @handle" capline exists to prevent.

User value review

What shipped shows that v9 removes from sight: nothing real. The five verdict costumes are one self-estimated integer (hooks-runner.ts:605) and the targeting line names an archetype whose receipt branch has never rendered. Both subtract trust rather than add value.

What v9 shows that shipped does not: the case-study cover (the moat made visible), the multiplier as an image badge, a dek that says shot + mechanism in one breath, and — in the brief — the audience's reaction frame, which is the actual brief the hook was written against and has never been on any card.

The one value trade to decide: on the idea card the creator's take now lives in the brief. If it must stay visible, it swaps with the source sentence on the face; everything else holds.
