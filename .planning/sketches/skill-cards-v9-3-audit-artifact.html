Skill cards·v9.2b audit· 1 Aug 2026·task/skill-cards-rework @ cc807d93

The card got shorter. The decision got harder.

v9.2b is measured against the real shipped renderers, mounted with matched fixtures — not a reproduction. The four-band geometry is right and the source row is the best component in the system. Three things stop it reading like a finished product surface: at 728 the card is roughly half empty, the open brief is still the typed-cell document the owner has now rejected twice, and the set of five lost every signal you would pick on. Nine fields drop out of the system that the handoff records as retained, and one wording lock states a number the engine does not compute.

03 — Findings

Visual design

Ordered by how much each one costs the "would Perplexity ship this" test.

V1

The card is a mobile layout inside a desktop object

blocks build

At 728 the hero is capped at 44ch and the dek at 58ch, so text stops around x=560 of a 688px content box. The source row's stat line ends at x≈420 and the chevron is pinned at 708 — 288px of nothing between a fact and its affordance. The brief row does the same. No band uses the right half of the card, and nothing balances it. This single fact is why the card reads unfinished at desktop width while looking correct at 342 — the layout was designed at 342 and stretched.

Fix directions in §4: narrow the object to its measure, give the face a fact rail, or make every row earn its right edge with a labeled affordance. All three fill the gutter without going near portrait-media-beside-text.

V2

The open brief re-proposes a treatment already rejected twice

blocks build

Five cells, each a cream sentence-case title over one muted line, stacked vertically with hairlines. That is v8's typed cells (rejected: "not clean and premium") crossed with v6's bold lead-ins (rejected: "reads like a document"). At 728 it is a 670px column of left-aligned text. The one structured grid in the whole system that the owner kept is the remix decode map — two columns, labeled cells, real matrix rules. The brief should speak that language, not this one.

Measured: the whole card with the brief open is 670 @728 / 843 @342 — taller at 342 than the shipped card it replaces. As a two-column map the brief body goes 341 → 298 at 728 while carrying one more fact, and the dead right half disappears. See §4.2 for the mobile cost.

V3

Sibling cards have ragged top edges

regression

Hook 1 renders Top pick + Copy; hook 2 renders Copy alone into the same 20px row. In a stack of five, one card has a chip and four have an empty band above the hero. The shipped card puts #1…#5 there, so every sibling's top edge carries the same weight and the row is never decorative.

V4

The script's timeline rail is cut at every beat

regression

v9.2b adds a full-bleed border-top between beats. The rail says "one continuous take"; the rules say "five separate rows". The shipped card has no per-beat rules — the rail alone carries the sequence, which is why it reads as a shot list. Drop the rules or inset them past the rail; do not keep both.

V5

Three glyph conventions for one control

craft

The brief chevron is › rotated 90° when open; script beat 1 uses a literal ⌄ while beats 2–5 use ›; the source row uses › to mean "leave the app". One caret, one rotation rule, and a distinct glyph for external.

V6

Save became an unlabeled glyph at the card's edge

craft

Shipped renders a bookmark and the word Save. v9.2b drops the label and pins the icon to the outer padding. At 342 the footer wraps and the bare icon is stranded on the primary's row while the door takes its own — three affordances, three visual weights, no grouping.

User experience

X1

The set of five lost everything you would choose on

blocks build

A hooks run delivers five cards and asks one question: which one. v9.2b removes rank, band, fraction, quote and mechanism from every face. What remains is identical on all five: a quoted line, the same source row, the same brief row, the same footer. To compare five cards you must open five briefs — 670px each. The design optimised the single card and regressed the unit the user actually operates on.

Cheapest honest fix, no engine work: restore the rank numeral, and put the taxonomy the schema already carries on the face as a chip — visualHook.technique for hooks ("crash-zoom", "match-cut"), format for ideas ("Talking-head"). Both are live fields the mock currently discards. Mocked in §4.4.

X2

The door is a one-way trip

blocks build

The footer has exactly one state: Not tested yet · Simulate with your audience →. Simulate, come back, and the card says "not tested yet" again. The handoff defers the measured state to ③, but without it the door cannot close a loop — and the whole reason the verdict left the face was that the door would own measurement. A door that owns measurement must be able to show one. Three states mocked in §4.3.

X3

Two forward CTAs compete in one row

regression

Write the script → and Simulate with your audience → are both arrow calls-to-action of similar length. At 342 the door takes a full row and reads louder than the primary. One of them is the chain step; the other is a diagnostic. They should not look like siblings.

X4

The brief's contents vanish exactly where staging needs defending

regression

body[data-w="342"] .n-brief .items{display:none} — at mobile width the row is a bare Full brief ›. Staging is a promise that the detail is one tap away and named; on the smaller screen the naming is the part that got cut. It is also the only special-case left in a design whose thesis is "one layout at every width".

X5

A chevron promises a drill-in and delivers a new tab

craft

The source row is an <a> to TikTok wearing ›, the same glyph the brief row uses to expand in place. Two different destinies, one affordance. Use ↗ and name it (Open on TikTok ↗) — which also gives the row's right edge something true to carry (see V1).

X6

Briefs stay open with no way to compare

craft

Open card 3, scroll, open card 4 — card 3 stays expanded and the stack grows by 670px per card. There is no "compare" or accordion behaviour. Either the top card arrives open and opening another closes it, or the brief cells need to be scannable enough that opening two is not a scroll event.

N1

"90.7× their usual reach" is not the number we compute

honesty

proof-schema.ts is explicit: multiplier is the durable outlier basis, views ÷ followers, and baselineLabel carries that basis so the card never shows a bare {n}×. "Their usual reach" asserts views ÷ their own median views — a different ratio, which nothing in the pipeline calculates. v9.2b drops the basis label and replaces it with a claim we cannot support. This is locked as final wording in handoff §5, so the lock itself is the defect.

Recommendation: 90.7× vs followers · 621K views, or if "vs followers" reads too technical, 90.7× this creator's follower count · 621K views. Never the reach paraphrase.

N2

One of the five brief cells will render empty in production

blocks build

"Made for 34% of your audience" comes from target, and its body line is reaction_frame — a field that does not exist on CardTargetSchema yet (it is one of the two additive engine follow-ups). All three generation runners also pass personas: [], so the existing verdict/quote branch has never rendered either. Ship v9.2b today and the brief is four cells and a title. Either the cell ships behind the engine change, or it degrades to something true.

N3

"Case study" is one row title doing two different jobs

regression

On a grounded hook the source was retrieved, scored against the audience and passed an outlier check — "Case study" is fair. On remix the user pasted the video: the schema comment is explicit that multiplier, baselineLabel and fitLabel all stay null because nothing measured it. The mock's remix row correctly says "The post you're remixing", but the shared component must derive the title from the data, never default to the stronger claim.

N4

The template lost its status

craft

The bracketed hookTemplate — "I stopped [common habit] for [timeframe]…" — is the most directly reusable object on the shipped card, and the one thing there that looks designed rather than written. In v9.2b it is the fourth line of a collapsed brief. If anything earns a place on the face beside the hero, it is this.

User flow

F1

The expensive step got harder and the cheap step got easier

regression

The flow is: run hooks → compare five → pick → write the script. Comparing is the expensive step and it is the one v9.2b stripped (X1). Writing the script was already a single button. Net effect on the funnel: the card is 35% shorter and the decision it exists to support is slower.

F2

Simulate has no return path

blocks build

Same root as X2. The door opens the room; the room reports; the card cannot receive the report. Until it can, "Not tested yet" is the only truthful thing it will ever say, and the verdict's removal from the face is a deletion rather than a relocation.

F3

No-source cards silently become a different product

craft

254px, three bands, no receipt — a clean object, exactly as intended. But at that size the face is a line and a dek, and it is indistinguishable from a chat message. Worth confirming what fraction of production cards land here: if it is common, the "complete object" argument needs the face to carry one more true thing.

05 — Locks

Five things the handoff records as settled that measurement contradicts

Each needs an explicit accept or reject before the build plan in §5 of the handoff can run — they are load-bearing for the components it names.

L1

§1 · "with them NOTHING real from shipped is lost"

false

Seven fields are absent, not staged: rank, topic, format, archetype, fitLabel, baselineLabel, channel. Three of them are the set's only differentiators.

L2

§5 · "90.7× their usual reach" never "vs followers"

unsupported

The engine computes views ÷ followers. The locked wording states a ratio nothing calculates, and drops the basis label the shipped card is required to show.

L3

§1 · "mobile is the desktop layout narrowed — no special-casing anywhere"

one exception

The brief row's contents list is display:none at 342.

L4

§1 · the brief's five cells, as the value fix

one cell has no data

"Made for N% of your audience" needs reaction_frame, which is a deferred engine follow-up. Four cells ship; the fifth is a heading.

L5

§2.7 · the extracted mechanism keyword is "obsolete"

re-open

It was retired because brief cells now have fixed titles — a decision about cells. The set-level differentiator problem (X1) is a different problem, and it is currently unsolved. It can be solved without that field, using visualHook.technique and format, which is what §4.4 does — but the lock should be re-read as "obsolete as a cell title", not "obsolete".

06 — Recommendation

What I would build, in order

Keep, unchanged

  1. The four-band geometry and the source row. The row is the strongest component in the system and it survives every direction above.
  2. Staging over deletion, and the verdict off the face.
  3. The no-source card as a complete 254px object.

Change before build

  1. Pick a width theory — B, C or D from §4.1. Nothing else in the audit matters as much to the "premium" reading, and the build plan cannot start without it.
  2. The brief becomes the map (§4.2). It fixes the rejected idiom and the dead right half, reuses a grid the owner already approved, and brings topic/format back. It is 43px shorter at 728 and ~100px taller at 342 — settle that trade before building.
  3. Restore the rank numeral and one taxonomy chip per card (§4.4). Zero engine work; it is the difference between a stack you can scan and five identical objects.
  4. Give the door three states (§4.3), or the verdict's removal is a deletion.
  5. Fix the multiplier wording (N1) and derive the row title from the data (N3).
  6. Decide the empty brief cell (N2): gate it behind reaction_frame, or ship four cells.

Cheap craft, any time

  1. One caret convention; ↗ for external (V5, X5).
  2. Drop the per-beat rules on the script timeline (V4).
  3. Label Save; group the footer as [primary + save] · [door] (V6, X3).
  4. Un-hide the brief's contents at 342 (X4, L3).
